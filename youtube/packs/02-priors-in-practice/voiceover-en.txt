Tutorial 01 gave you reinvent_pubchem.prior. Downloading it was not a research decision. This episode is.

Official docs list prior filenames. Lab work asks: will this generative starting point cover the chemistry you care about? If your project is sulfonamide-rich — or macrocycles, peptides, covalent warheads — and the prior almost never emits that motif, reinforcement learning burns its first hours rediscovering it. Or never finds it.

Many Zenodo files are different generators — Mol2Mol, LibInvent — different tasks. A fair test keeps the Reinvent generator fixed and changes only the weights. So we compare reinvent_pubchem.prior against a short transfer-learning checkpoint: tl_sulfonamide.model, fine-tuned eight epochs on sulfonamides. Same architecture. New distribution.

Protocol is the science. CPU. Seed 42. About two hundred SMILES each. Unique molecules on. Two config files that differ only in model file and output file. Transfer learning on this demo set finished in about thirteen seconds and peaked near 871 megabytes of RAM. After uniqueness you get slightly fewer than 200 rows — 191 versus 196 in the handbook run. That is expected.

Score the decision with RDKit. SMARTS: S equals O twice N. PubChem prior: 8.4 percent sulfonamide hits. After short transfer learning: 64.3 percent. Unique Murcko scaffolds stay high — 168 versus 172. Mean QED moves from 0.59 to 0.65 as a side effect, not the goal. Short transfer learning shifted token preference toward the sulfonamide motif without collapsing scaffold diversity in this seed-42 draw.

What next? If the prior already hits your SMARTS at an acceptable rate, stay on PubChem and go score in Tutorial 03. If the prior rarely hits and transfer learning enriches without killing scaffolds, use that checkpoint as your starting agent or prior for reinforcement learning. Need linkers, R-groups, or analogue-by-seed? Wrong generator — not more PubChem epochs. Peptides or exotic elements: check token support.

How was the sulfonamide set built? Two sampling jobs on the PubChem prior, SMARTS match, deduplicate, then a train-validation split: 145 train, 10 hold-out. You can rebuild it. Using the shipped files keeps this episode aligned with the published figure. sample_batch_size must be at least 100 or transfer learning fails validation before it starts. An invalid-hash warning on the prior is informational in current builds — training still proceeds.

Mean NLL fell from 35.95 to 29.48 after short TL. Validity stayed 100 percent in both unique samples. Higher QED after TL is a side effect, not a goal. Separate the chemotype you wanted from properties that moved along for the ride. Keep randomize_smiles true on both legs so the comparison is not confounded by SMILES augmentation.

Priors are experimental choices. Compare under one protocol. Measure chemotype hit rate and scaffolds. Decide with evidence before you burn reinforcement-learning compute.
